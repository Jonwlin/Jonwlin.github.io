A few notes: the scallion oil is the whole dish, so don't rush it — low and slow
until the scallions are deep brown and crisp is what gives it that intense
Shanghai-restaurant flavor. Stop just before they blacken; burnt scallions turn
the oil bitter. The sauce is intense, so start small — spoon just a little over
each portion and add more to taste. Pat the scallions thoroughly dry before frying
to cut down on splattering. For the most authentic 葱油开洋拌面, add the dried
shrimp (kai yang); ground pork makes it a heartier meal. Leftover scallion oil and
sauce both keep in the fridge for a faster meal later.
